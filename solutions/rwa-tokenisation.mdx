## Buyer problem

Tokenised real-world assets bring institutional-grade obligations onto blockchain rails. Issuers, transfer agents and platforms need an explainable control point at the moment of issuance, redemption or transfer, not after value has moved. Existing off-chain controls are strong, but the on-chain moment of settlement is where risk crystallises.

## Cardinal solution

Cardinal adds an explainable decision layer at the moment of on-chain action. It evaluates transaction intent and returns a scored ALLOW, REVIEW, or BLOCK decision that your issuance, redemption and transfer flows can enforce, log and audit.

## Example protected workflow

<Steps>
  <Step title="Investor requests issuance, redemption or transfer">
    Your platform captures the request and prepares the on-chain action.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Your backend calls Cardinal">
    Before signing or settlement, the Protection API is called with the transaction intent and context.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Cardinal returns an explainable decision">
    ALLOW, REVIEW or BLOCK is returned with human-readable findings for issuers, transfer agents and compliance.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Your workflow enforces the outcome">
    REVIEW or BLOCK decisions route into transfer-agent, compliance or legal workflows with full context.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Every decision is logged">
    Outcomes are retained for internal audit, transfer-agent records and regulator-facing reporting.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Capability boundary

<Warning>
  Cardinal does not replace KYC, KYB, AML, sanctions, source-of-funds review, custody, legal or title review, or licensed escrow. Pre-transaction decisioning is available today. Incoming-funds tracing and stolen or hacked-funds detection are on the roadmap. ALLOW is not a safety guarantee.
</Warning>

## Pilot starting point

A typical RWA pilot scopes one flow (for example, issuance or redemption over a threshold), agrees measures with the issuer and transfer agent, and runs for 60 to 90 days. Final scope and commercial terms are agreement-specific.
