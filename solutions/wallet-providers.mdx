## Buyer problem

Wallets are the last line of defence before a user signs. When approval prompts are opaque, users approve transactions they do not fully understand. When users lose funds, they blame the wallet, and support and reputation take the hit.

## Cardinal solution

Cardinal is called before your wallet's sign prompt. It evaluates intent and returns an explainable ALLOW, REVIEW, or BLOCK decision alongside findings your UI can render. Cardinal never requests private keys or seed phrases and never takes custody.

## Example protected workflow

<Steps>
  <Step title="User initiates a transaction">
    Your wallet prepares the transaction intent from the dApp interaction or user input.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Wallet calls Cardinal before signing">
    The Protection API is called with the transaction intent, counterparties and context.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Cardinal returns an explainable decision">
    ALLOW, REVIEW or BLOCK is returned with human-readable findings the wallet UI can render.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Wallet renders a clearer prompt">
    Users see a plain-language risk summary. REVIEW and BLOCK decisions can pause signing and surface the reason.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Wallet enforces and logs the outcome">
    Signing only proceeds when your policy allows, and the decision is retained for support and audit.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Capability boundary

<Warning>
  Cardinal never requests private keys or seed phrases and does not take custody of user funds. Pre-transaction decisioning is available today. Incoming-funds tracing and stolen or hacked-funds detection are on the roadmap.
</Warning>
