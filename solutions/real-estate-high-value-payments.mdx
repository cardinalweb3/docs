## Buyer problem

High-value payments (property completions, OTC settlements, corporate transfers) are one-off, high-stakes events. A single error, misdirection or fraudulent instruction can be catastrophic, and the parties involved often want a documented safety window before funds are final.

## Cardinal solution

Cardinal combines an explainable pre-transaction check with protected settlement patterns. High-value flows can be routed through Cardinal's cancellable or controlled-release settlement rails, so both sides have a documented, auditable window between decision and finality.

## Example protected workflow

<Steps>
  <Step title="Parties agree the payment">
    Your platform prepares the payment, counterparties and required approvals.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Your platform calls Cardinal">
    Before signing, the Protection API is called with the transaction intent and context.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Cardinal returns an explainable decision">
    ALLOW, REVIEW or BLOCK is returned with human-readable findings for operators, counterparties and audit.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Protected settlement, where appropriate">
    For high-value flows, transfers can be routed through SafeSend or Programmable Escrow for a documented safety window.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Decisions and outcomes are logged">
    Every step is retained for legal review, audit and post-transaction reporting.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Capability boundary

<Warning>
  Cardinal does not replace licensed escrow, legal or title review, KYC, KYB, AML, sanctions or source-of-funds review. SafeSend is a controlled Arbitrum Sepolia testnet MVP and Programmable Escrow is in final testing and security hardening; availability is agreement-specific. ALLOW is not a safety guarantee.
</Warning>

## Pilot starting point

A real estate or high-value payment pilot typically scopes one payment type (for example, deposits, completions, or OTC settlements over a defined threshold), agrees measures with the operator and counterparties, and runs for 60 to 90 days. Final scope and commercial terms are agreement-specific.
