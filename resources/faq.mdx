Use this guide to understand Cardinal's current capabilities and resolve common integration and pilot issues. Work through the relevant section before contacting support — most issues can be diagnosed with the checklist at the bottom of this page.

<Warning>
  Never include API keys, private keys, seed phrases, passwords, authentication tokens, identity documents, or confidential transaction records in screenshots, logs, or support requests.
</Warning>

## General

<Accordion title="What is Cardinal?">
  Cardinal is security infrastructure for digital-asset transactions. It evaluates a proposed transaction before signature or settlement and returns an explainable `ALLOW`, `REVIEW`, or `BLOCK` decision based on the intent you submit. Partners combine this result with internal policy, approvals, SafeSend, or Programmable Escrow to control how a transaction proceeds.

  Instead of returning only a risk score, Cardinal connects pre-transaction decisioning to the way a transaction settles — you can continue, pause for review, require additional approval, route through a protected transfer, route into Escrow, or stop entirely.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Is Cardinal custodial?">
  No. Cardinal is designed as non-custodial, trust-minimised infrastructure. You and your authorised operators retain control of wallets and signing. Cardinal never asks for private keys or seed phrases and never signs transactions on a user's behalf.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Does Cardinal guarantee that a transaction is safe?">
  No. Cardinal reduces risk using the information and controls available at the time of the check, but it cannot guarantee complete protection. You and your users remain responsible for verifying transaction details, managing keys, applying approvals, and deciding whether to proceed.

  <Tip>
    Implement a fail-closed policy: treat API errors, timeouts, and service unavailability as `BLOCK` — never as `ALLOW`.
  </Tip>
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Does Cardinal replace KYC, AML, or sanctions screening?">
  No. Cardinal does not replace KYC, KYB, AML, KYT, sanctions screening, source-of-funds or source-of-wealth checks, regulated custody, legal review, title verification, or licensed escrow. You remain responsible for all applicable compliance obligations.

  Incoming-funds tracing and stolen or hacked-funds detection remain roadmap capabilities and must not be treated as active screening services.
</Accordion>

## Protection API

<Accordion title="When should I call the Protection API?">
  Call the API after the complete transaction intent is known and immediately before the wallet signature, broadcast, SafeSend funding, or Escrow release. Always make this call from your trusted backend — never from the client.

  Re-check when any of the following change: sender, recipient, network, token, amount, contract, approval scope, permissions, or transaction type.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="What do ALLOW, REVIEW, and BLOCK mean?">
  | Decision | Meaning | Minimum required action |
  |---|---|---|
  | `ALLOW` | No blocking condition was returned for the submitted intent | Continue only if your own controls also pass |
  | `REVIEW` | The intent needs additional attention | Pause automatic execution; require explicit user acknowledgment before proceeding |
  | `BLOCK` | A blocking condition was returned | Stop the proposed transaction immediately |

  An `ALLOW` decision is not a warranty and does not replace your security and compliance controls.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Can I override a BLOCK decision?">
  No. Your integration must not silently weaken a `BLOCK`. Stop the proposed intent, preserve the evidence, and follow your documented investigation process. If a new or corrected intent is created, submit it as a separate, fresh check. A `REVIEW` decision requires explicit user acknowledgment before you allow the flow to continue.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Should I cache decisions?">
  Never reuse a decision for a changed intent. Decisions are point-in-time — always call the API fresh before signature or release. For higher-value transactions, apply a short partner-defined freshness window and re-check immediately when required by your policy.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="What happens if the API is unavailable?">
  Treat unavailability as fail-closed, not as `ALLOW`. Keep the transaction paused, queue it safely, and retry with exponential backoff once the service recovers. Never substitute an automatic `ALLOW` for an error, timeout, or unavailable response.
</Accordion>

## Troubleshooting

<Accordion title="I receive a 401 authentication error">
  Check the following:

  1. Your API key is present only in your trusted backend — never in client-side code.
  2. You are sending the key in the correct `x-api-key` header.
  3. The key belongs to the correct environment (pilot vs. production).
  4. The key has no extra spaces, quotation marks, or line breaks.
  5. The credential has not expired, been revoked, or been rotated.

  Log only a non-secret credential reference and the response status — never log the full key.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="I receive a 429 rate limit error">
  Your integration has exceeded the rate limit. Take these steps:

  - Pause automatic retries immediately.
  - Apply exponential backoff with jitter before retrying.
  - Check the `X-RateLimit` response headers for reset timing.
  - Prevent duplicate signing or settlement during the backoff period.
  - Review your traffic for accidental loops or repeated checks on the same intent.

  Do not bypass Cardinal or substitute an automatic `ALLOW` during rate limiting.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="The request times out">
  Use a short, bounded timeout appropriate to your workflow. When a timeout occurs:

  - Keep the transaction paused — do not allow the user to sign an unchecked intent.
  - Retry only when your policy permits, using exponential backoff.
  - Make the internal operation idempotent to prevent duplicate settlement.
  - Escalate sustained latency through the agreed support channel.

  Treat a timeout as fail-closed, not as `ALLOW`.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="I receive an unexpected BLOCK or REVIEW decision">
  First confirm that the submitted intent exactly matches the proposed transaction. Then record and preserve:

  - Your internal transaction ID
  - The Cardinal `request_id`
  - The canonical intent hash
  - Non-secret request fields
  - The decision and full `findings` array — this identifies the specific reason
  - Timestamp and expected result with your reasoning

  Do not resubmit modified data simply to obtain a preferred result. If the result still appears incorrect after reviewing the findings, send the non-secret evidence through the agreed support channel.
</Accordion>

## Networks & Tokens

<Accordion title="What chains does Cardinal support?">
  The current documented network slugs are:

  - `ethereum`
  - `bnb`
  - `polygon`
  - `base`
  - `arbitrum`
  - `optimism`
  - `solana`

  An accepted slug does not mean that every asset, feature, contract, jurisdiction, or settlement route is production-approved on that network. Confirm the exact environment and product status for your integration.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="What if a token is not recognised?">
  Confirm the network, token symbol spelling, contract address, decimals, and asset support for your specific environment. Token symbols are not globally unique — use the verified contract address whenever applicable.

  An unsupported or unverified token may trigger a finding in the response. Do not proceed with a lookalike or unverified token contract.
</Accordion>

## SafeSend

<Accordion title="What is SafeSend?">
  SafeSend is Cardinal's time-locked, cancellable ERC-20 transfer mechanism. It introduces a protection window before a transfer becomes final, giving the sender the ability to cancel if something looks wrong. SafeSend integrates directly with the Protection API so every transfer is checked before it is funded.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Is SafeSend live on mainnet?">
  No. The currently verified SafeSend flow is a controlled Arbitrum Sepolia testnet MVP. Do not use it with unrestricted production funds or describe it as generally available on mainnet until the exact deployment and readiness status are confirmed for your integration.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Can I cancel a SafeSend transfer?">
  Yes — but only before the `releaseTime` is reached, and only the original sender can cancel. A transfer cannot be assumed cancellable after it becomes claimable, claimed, expired, or otherwise reaches a terminal contract state. Your interface must show the current transfer state and the actions permitted in that state.
</Accordion>

## Escrow

<Accordion title="Is Programmable Escrow production-ready?">
  Not yet. Programmable Escrow remains in final testing and security hardening. Do not describe it as audited, licensed, regulated, unrestricted, or generally available on mainnet until those facts are confirmed for the exact deployment you are using.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Who controls the funds in an Escrow?">
  The smart contract controls the funds — neither Cardinal nor any single party holds them unilaterally. The applicable contract roles determine who can fund, approve, release, refund, cancel, or dispute. The primary roles are buyer, seller, and dispute resolver.

  Cardinal is not a substitute for a regulated escrow provider where one is legally required.
</Accordion>

## Pilot

<Accordion title="How long is a Cardinal pilot?">
  A typical design-partner pilot runs for **60–90 days**. The signed pilot plan defines the exact duration, scope, transaction values, participants, success criteria, and commercial terms.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Can I use real funds during the pilot?">
  For the Protection API: yes, but only within the boundaries of your signed pilot scope, after all security, legal, and compliance approvals are in place, and subject to agreed value limits.

  For SafeSend: testnet funds only. The current SafeSend MVP runs on Arbitrum Sepolia with test USDC. Do not use mainnet funds with SafeSend until a production deployment is confirmed.
</Accordion>

## Diagnostic checklist

Work through these steps before contacting support:

- [ ] Confirm the current product and environment
- [ ] Confirm the network, token, contract address, and wallet
- [ ] Verify the final canonical intent you submitted
- [ ] Record the Cardinal `request_id` from the response
- [ ] Record your internal transaction ID and intent hash
- [ ] Capture the HTTP response status and non-secret error details
- [ ] Check the `findings` array for the specific reason behind a decision
- [ ] Confirm whether the issue is reproducible
- [ ] Check whether another request or blockchain transaction is already pending
- [ ] Remove all secrets and personal data from any evidence before sharing
