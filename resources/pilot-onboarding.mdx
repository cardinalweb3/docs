Cardinal pilots help you validate pre-transaction protection and risk-routed settlement in a controlled environment before wider production adoption. A typical design-partner pilot runs for **60–90 days**, focuses on one clearly defined transaction flow, targets a limited group of users, operates within explicit network and asset boundaries, and tracks measurable success criteria throughout.

<Warning>
  A Cardinal pilot is not approval for unrestricted production use. SafeSend is currently a controlled Arbitrum Sepolia testnet MVP. Programmable Escrow remains in final testing and security hardening. The Protection API does not replace KYC, KYB, AML, sanctions, custody, legal, licensing, or regulated-escrow processes.
</Warning>

## Who the program is for

The program is designed for organisations that can contribute a real workflow, an accountable implementation team, and structured feedback. Target organisations include:

- Wallet and exchange providers
- Custodians and vault platforms
- Stablecoin and payment companies
- RWA and tokenisation platforms
- UAE real estate brokers and developers
- OTC desks and enterprise treasury teams
- Luxury vehicle, yacht, and private-aviation businesses
- Other businesses processing high-value digital-asset payments

<Card title="Recommended starting point" icon="flag-checkered">
  Start with one real high-value customer workflow and one wallet, exchange, or digital-asset platform integration. Keep each pilot narrow enough to measure clearly.
</Card>

## Pilot outcomes

Every pilot should answer five questions:

1. Can Cardinal be integrated into the selected workflow safely?
2. Does pre-transaction decisioning improve the handling of risky or incorrect intents?
3. Can you operate `ALLOW`, `REVIEW`, `BLOCK`, error, and unavailable outcomes consistently?
4. Can the transaction, decision, approval, and settlement evidence be reconciled?
5. Is there a justified path to a production commercial agreement?

The pilot is complete only when technical results and your security, compliance, operational, and commercial acceptance criteria have all been reviewed.

## Onboarding phases

<Steps>
  <Step title="Qualification">
    Cardinal and your team confirm the transaction or payment workflow, the business problem and current control gap, expected transaction volume and value range, target network and assets, required decision and approval points, named owners across business, technical, security, compliance, and operations, required product capabilities, legal and regulatory dependencies, and your target launch window.

    Avoid pilots that depend on unverified roadmap features or an unrestricted production deployment. Narrow the scope until it can be supported and tested safely.

    **Primary output:** Pilot brief
  </Step>

  <Step title="Scope & Controls">
    Create a written pilot plan before integration begins. Document your partner name and pilot owners, selected use case and user journey, included and excluded products, network and token and value limits, number and type of pilot users, approved addresses and contracts, and pilot dates and commercial terms.

    Agree how every decision outcome will be handled:

    | Outcome | Required pilot action |
    |---|---|
    | `ALLOW` | Continue only when all your controls also pass |
    | `REVIEW` | Pause and follow your named manual-review or additional-approval process |
    | `BLOCK` | Stop the proposed transaction and record the case |
    | Error or timeout | Fail closed and pause or queue the transaction |
    | Cardinal unavailable | Apply your agreed degraded-service process — never silently allow |

    Your policy may be stricter than Cardinal's. A pilot must never weaken a `BLOCK` result.

    **Primary output:** Agreed pilot plan
  </Step>

  <Step title="Technical Setup">
    Use separate pilot credentials and an approved non-production or limited pilot environment.

    - Keep the Cardinal API key behind your backend
    - Store secrets in an approved secret manager
    - Restrict access using least privilege
    - Do not copy production secrets or unnecessary personal data into the pilot
    - Confirm supported network and asset identifiers
    - Confirm exact smart-contract addresses from an approved source
    - Use test assets where the selected product is not production-ready

    Your integration should: build the final canonical transaction intent, submit it to the Protection API from a trusted backend, store the Cardinal `request_id`, decision, findings, and intent hash, apply your agreed decision policy, bind user confirmation to the checked intent, verify that the signed transaction matches the intent, and reconcile the final blockchain result.

    Read the [Integration Guide](/guides/integration-overview) and [Security Best Practices](/security/best-practices) before implementation.

    **Primary output:** Staging integration
  </Step>

  <Step title="Validation">
    Complete your agreed test plan before the controlled launch. You must cover all 13 required decision tests:

    1. Valid low-risk intent
    2. First-time or changed recipient
    3. Wrong network
    4. Changed token or amount
    5. Changed contract or permission scope
    6. `REVIEW` handling
    7. `BLOCK` handling
    8. Invalid request
    9. Authentication failure
    10. Rate limit response
    11. Timeout and unavailable service
    12. Duplicate submission or retry
    13. Checked-intent and signed-transaction mismatch

    Before launch, confirm that test cases pass (or have approved limitations), pilot users are trained, value and address limits are enforced, monitoring and escalation contacts are active, incident and credential-rotation procedures are documented, and all legal, compliance, security, and operational approvals are recorded.

    **Primary output:** Readiness record
  </Step>

  <Step title="Controlled Launch">
    Begin with the smallest useful cohort and transaction value. Apply these launch controls throughout:

    - Named pilot users only
    - Explicit transaction and daily value limits
    - Approved networks, tokens, destinations, and contracts
    - Manual handling for every `REVIEW` outcome
    - No bypass for `BLOCK`, timeout, or unavailable outcomes
    - Dual approval for high-value or release actions where required
    - Daily reconciliation during the initial launch period
    - A documented pause procedure

    Expand the pilot only after the current stage meets its acceptance criteria. A new network, asset, product, or transaction type may require a separate validation cycle.

    **Primary output:** Pilot transactions
  </Step>

  <Step title="Evaluation">
    At the end of the pilot, Cardinal and your team review results against every success criterion, confirmed product and operational value, security and compliance and reliability gaps, required remediation and owners, production scope and commercial terms, and whether a case study or announcement is separately approved.

    Possible outcomes are: proceed to a production agreement, extend the controlled pilot with documented objectives, pause for remediation or product readiness, or end the pilot without production deployment.

    Pilot participation does not create permission to use Cardinal branding, publish results, or make security, audit, compliance, or production claims unless agreed in writing.

    **Primary output:** Pilot outcome report
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Responsibility matrix

| Area | Cardinal | Partner |
|---|---|---|
| Protection API | Operates and provides integration guidance | Implements and validates |
| API key management | Issues credentials | Secures and rotates |
| Decision enforcement | Recommends `ALLOW`, `REVIEW`, or `BLOCK` | Enforces the agreed decision policy |
| Settlement contracts | Provides testnet MVP | Operates within agreed scope |
| User identity and authority | Not the identity provider | Responsible |
| KYC / AML / sanctions | Not a replacement | Partner responsibility |
| Legal, title, and licensing | Provides product information | Responsible |
| Incident management | Supports product investigation | Owns customer and regulatory response |

## Success metrics

Agree baselines, targets, data sources, owners, and review dates before launch.

| Category | Example measurements |
|---|---|
| Integration | Time to integrate, test completion, deployment readiness |
| Decisioning | `ALLOW`, `REVIEW`, and `BLOCK` distribution; confirmed useful findings |
| Accuracy | False-positive rate, false-negative investigations, reviewer agreement |
| Operations | Review handling time, approval time, escalation volume |
| Reliability | API latency, availability, errors, timeouts, retry success |
| Security | Wrong-network or altered-intent prevention, policy bypass attempts |
| Reconciliation | Requests matched to business records and transaction hashes |
| Customer impact | User completion, abandonment, support contacts, protected value |

<Note>
  Do not claim fraud-loss reduction without a defensible baseline and verified outcome data.
</Note>

## Onboarding checklist

Work through all 13 items before your controlled launch:

- [ ] Pilot brief and accountable owners confirmed
- [ ] Use case, scope, exclusions, duration, and commercial terms agreed
- [ ] Network, token, values, addresses, contracts, and users limited
- [ ] Responsibility matrix approved
- [ ] Success metrics and baselines documented
- [ ] Pilot credentials issued and stored securely
- [ ] Integration and security guidance reviewed
- [ ] Decision, error, timeout, and unavailable paths tested
- [ ] All 13 required decision tests completed
- [ ] Product lifecycle tests completed where applicable
- [ ] Legal, compliance, security, and operational approvals recorded
- [ ] Monitoring, support, escalation, and incident contacts active
- [ ] Controlled-launch approval signed off and weekly review scheduled
