Cardinal helps custodians and vault operators place an explainable transaction-control layer between a transfer proposal and final signing. The Protection API evaluates the canonical intent, returns `ALLOW`, `REVIEW`, or `BLOCK`, and enables your policy engine to select direct settlement, additional approval, SafeSend, or Programmable Escrow. The result is a documented, auditable decision record that sits alongside your existing quorum and signing controls.

<Warning>
  Cardinal does not custody assets, hold private keys, replace your signing system, or authorise a transfer on your behalf. SafeSend is currently a connected Arbitrum Sepolia testnet MVP. Programmable Escrow remains in final testing and security hardening.
</Warning>

## 7-step vault workflow

<Steps>
  <Step title="Transaction proposal">
    A user, authorised policy process, or integrated system creates a transfer proposal. The proposal captures the source vault or sub-account, recipient, network, token, amount, and any contract interaction details. Lock the canonical payload at this stage so that approvers and signers evaluate the same intent that Cardinal checked.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Authentication and validation">
    Your platform authenticates the requester and validates account, vault, mandate, and destination rules before the Cardinal check runs. Confirm that the proposer has authority to initiate the proposed transfer, and that the recipient and value fall within approved limits.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Cardinal check from the server-side adapter">
    A server-side Cardinal adapter submits the final transaction intent to the Protection API. Never call Cardinal from a browser, mobile client, or signing infrastructure. Only the server adapter should hold the Cardinal API key.

    ```json
    {
      "from_address": "0xInstitutionalVault",
      "to_address": "0xApprovedCounterparty",
      "chain": "ethereum",
      "token": "USDC",
      "amount": "500000",
      "transaction_type": "transfer"
    }
    ```

    Required fields are `from_address`, `to_address`, `chain`, `token`, and `amount`. Include `contract_address`, `contract_verified`, `approval_amount`, and `permissions` when the proposed action involves a contract call or token approval.

    Generate or preserve an internal intent hash from the canonical payload. Store it with the Cardinal `request_id`, policy result, approval record, and signed transaction.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Policy engine applies the decision">
    Your policy engine combines the Cardinal decision with internal controls — velocity rules, destination allowlists, value thresholds, and vault-specific mandates.

    | Decision | Custodian Action |
    |---|---|
    | ALLOW | Normal quorum signing — continue only when all internal controls pass |
    | REVIEW | Enhanced review — pause automated processing, require additional signer or protected settlement |
    | BLOCK | Reject and quarantine — stop key release, signing, and broadcast |

    Internal policy may be stricter than Cardinal. Never weaken a `BLOCK` decision or treat a timeout, invalid response, or unavailable service as `ALLOW`.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Approver review">
    Required approvers review the same immutable intent and Cardinal findings before authorising. For `ALLOW` decisions, the configured quorum proceeds. For `REVIEW` decisions, require enhanced review, an additional signer, or a protected settlement route. Bind every approval to the intent hash and Cardinal `request_id` so the approval cannot be detached and reused.
  </Step>

  <Step title="HSM, MPC, or multisig signing">
    Your existing HSM, MPC, multisig, or wallet infrastructure signs and broadcasts only after every control passes — authentication, Cardinal check, policy engine, and quorum approval. Cardinal never needs access to private keys, seed phrases, HSM credentials, MPC shares, or raw signing material. Keep Cardinal API credentials completely separate from custody keys and signing infrastructure.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Record and reconcile">
    Record the transaction hash and final settlement state. Reconcile the on-chain outcome with the proposal record, Cardinal `request_id`, approval log, and signing request. Flag any interrupted workflow so a stale approval cannot reach signing.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Separation of duties

Cardinal complements, not replaces, institutional controls. Enforce the following boundaries in your integration:

- Keep proposal creation separate from approval and signing.
- Require the configured quorum for the vault, asset, value, and destination.
- Prevent the proposer from altering the intent after approval.
- Bind every approval to the intent hash and Cardinal decision.
- Require fresh approval when recipient, chain, token, amount, contract, permissions, or route changes.
- Record emergency overrides with named authority, reason, timestamp, and independent review.
- Keep Cardinal API credentials separate from custody keys and signing infrastructure.

## Protected settlement routes

Your policy engine selects the settlement path based on the Cardinal verdict and your internal controls:

- **Direct settlement** — normal custodian signing flow after an `ALLOW` decision and all internal approvals pass.
- **Additional approval** — require a higher quorum, specialist review, or delayed signing for `REVIEW` decisions.
- **SafeSend** — route an eligible pilot transfer through a delay or cancellation-window flow. The verified integration is currently on Arbitrum Sepolia testnet.
- **Programmable Escrow** — use an agreed pilot flow where release conditions, cancellation, or counterparty approval are required.
- **Stop** — prevent key release, signing, and broadcast after every `BLOCK`.

Confirm contract addresses, supported production networks, fees, audit status, and custody compatibility in your partner implementation plan before routing institutional value.

## Vault and treasury use cases

A pilot can begin with one or more of the following controlled scenarios:

- Treasury transfers to approved counterparties
- New or changed withdrawal destinations
- High-value or unusual asset movements
- OTC or institutional payment settlement
- Token approvals and permission changes
- Smart-contract interactions initiated from a managed vault
- Transfers that require enhanced review or protected settlement

<Note>
  Cardinal is a transaction decision and routing layer. Vault strategy, yield risk, protocol solvency, portfolio construction, custody licensing, and mandate suitability remain outside the current public integration scope. Cardinal does not custody assets or private keys at any point in the workflow.
</Note>

<Warning>
  The current Protection API provides pre-transaction decisioning and deterministic checks for the submitted intent. It is not a complete AML, KYT, sanctions, custody-compliance, protocol-risk, or stolen-funds screening system. Incoming-funds analysis, source-of-funds tracing, illicit-exposure detection, sanctions screening, and one-to-two-hop exposure analysis are roadmap capabilities. Your custodian or vault operation remains responsible for custody controls, customer due diligence, mandate enforcement, AML policy, key governance, and all regulatory obligations.
</Warning>

## Audit and reconciliation

Store the following evidence fields for every transaction to reconstruct the complete control path:

| Field | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Proposal, vault, account, and workflow IDs | Link the Cardinal check to the internal proposal |
| Cardinal `request_id`, decision, findings, and `checked_at` | Record the specific API result |
| Canonical intent and intent hash | Prove what Cardinal evaluated and bind approvals |
| Internal policy result and selected settlement route | Record any stricter partner-side decision |
| Approver identities, roles, timestamps, and quorum outcome | Evidence of human review |
| Override, acknowledgement, or manual-review evidence | Document exceptions |
| Signing request ID, transaction hash, and broadcast time | Join decisioning to on-chain execution |
| Final on-chain status and reconciliation result | Confirm settlement |
| Re-check history and superseded intent versions | Track changes |

Avoid recording API keys, custody secrets, private keys, MPC shares, full authentication tokens, or unnecessary personal data.

## Pilot checklist

Before enabling a custodian or vault workflow, confirm the following:

- [ ] Agree on the vaults, assets, networks, transaction types, and value limits in scope.
- [ ] Document the proposal, review, approval, signing, broadcast, and reconciliation boundaries.
- [ ] Keep the Cardinal API key in a secret manager and rotate it under an agreed procedure.
- [ ] Confirm Cardinal cannot access custody keys or signing material.
- [ ] Test `ALLOW`, `REVIEW`, `BLOCK`, timeout, `429`, and unavailable-service paths end to end.
- [ ] Verify intent hashing, re-checks, expiry, quorum, and separation of duties.
- [ ] Test destination changes, contract calls, approval changes, and emergency overrides.
- [ ] Reconcile Cardinal `request_id` values with proposal IDs, signing requests, and transaction hashes.
- [ ] Confirm SafeSend or Escrow addresses, audit status, fees, networks, and production readiness separately.
- [ ] Complete custodian security, legal, AML, licensing, and jurisdiction-specific review.

<Tip>
  Start with one vault, one network, a narrow asset set, explicit value limits, and manual handling for all `REVIEW` decisions. Expand only after decision quality, latency, approval integrity, signing controls, and settlement reconciliation meet your agreed acceptance criteria.
</Tip>
