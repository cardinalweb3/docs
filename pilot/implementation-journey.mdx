Every pilot is agreement-specific, but most Cardinal design-partner pilots follow the same phases. The example below shows how a typical 60 to 90 day pilot is structured.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Discovery">
    We map your target workflow, existing controls and success criteria. Product, operations, risk and compliance stakeholders are aligned before we start.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Scope and sign">
    We agree written scope, measures, timelines and commercial terms. This includes what is in and out of the pilot, and how we will measure it.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Integration">
    Cardinal is wired into the target workflow. This is typically a small, well-defined integration point rather than a broad platform change.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Controlled rollout">
    The integration goes live in a controlled manner, often behind a flag or on a subset of traffic. Cardinal and your team monitor outcomes daily.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Review and decide">
    At the end of the pilot window we review outcomes against the agreed measures and decide together on the next step: broader rollout, extended pilot, or wind-down.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Who is involved

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Your side" icon="users">
    A product owner, an operations or risk lead, a technical integrator, and compliance sign-off.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Our side" icon="user-tie">
    A dedicated Cardinal contact plus product and engineering support scoped to the pilot.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

## What we do not commit to

<Note>
  Support and response commitments are agreement-specific. We do not publish 24/7 support or fixed SLAs here. Timelines, coverage and commercial terms are agreed in writing before a pilot starts.
</Note>
